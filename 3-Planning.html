Executive summary

During Watermark’s merger and rebrand, we shaped a next-generation assessment and accreditation platform for higher education. The opportunity was to make institutional planning and evidence collection easier to coordinate across a complex academic hierarchy.

The challenge

Assessment planning involves many people with distinct responsibilities, but the resulting evidence must still roll up into a coherent institutional story. The experience had to support local autonomy without losing shared visibility and accountability.

Reframing the problem

We framed planning as a living collaboration system—not a collection of forms. Goals, contributors, evidence, and reporting cadence need to remain connected as work moves between institutional levels.

Who we are designing for

Planning Role

Universities and colleges usually have a multi-level organization hierachy. Assessment Coordinator or Unit Administrator in an institution is usaually the creator of assessment plans.

Each level has their goals and objectives. Assessment Leads are the contact people for each organization. They are responsible for entering the objectives assessment results during each assessment period. These assessment results can be rolled over to support higher level organization's objective assessment.

In each program, faculty members work as assessment contributors, to score students' assignments, work, performance and provide these data to their Assessment Lead.
